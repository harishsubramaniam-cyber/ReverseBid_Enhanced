A reverse auction runs the opposite way to a normal one: you publish the most you will pay, and suppliers compete by going lower. The lowest price wins.

{% endif %}

The words we use

Starting price
The most you will pay per unit. No bid can be above it. It is optional — leave it empty and bidders open at whatever price they like.
Decrement
How much lower each new bid has to be than the current best. It stops bidders shaving off one rupee at a time.
L1, L2, L3
L1 is the lowest price right now — the winning position. L2 is second lowest, and so on.
Auto-extension
A bid in the last moments pushes the clock back, so nobody wins by bidding at the buzzer.
Baseline & savings
Baseline is quantity × starting price. Savings is baseline minus what you actually awarded — before you award, the best bids stand in. Where no starting price was set, the highest bid received is the baseline.
Who wins an item
One bidder per item, for the whole quantity. Different items can go to different bidders, or the lot to one — but an item is never split between two suppliers.
